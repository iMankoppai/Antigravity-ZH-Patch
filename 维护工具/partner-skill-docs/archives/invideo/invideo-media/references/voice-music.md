# Voice, music and sound

## What makes it work

Viewers credit the picture for what the sound did, so sound works best when nobody notices it. At every moment there is one thing to listen to, and everything else gets out of the way. Sound and picture take turns; they never say the same thing at once. Taking sound away is as strong as adding it.

- Decide who leads each section: the voice, the music or the effects.
- Two things of one kind at most, like a voice and a lyric. To add a layer, remove one.
- Quiet makes loud. Plan the valleys.

## Craft

**Voice.** Narration says what the picture can't: why, what came before, what is off frame. Never "here we see". One idea per breath. The read sounds like telling one person a story: project less, and read emotional lines flatter. Keep natural breaths; cut them for clarity, never for speed. Leave a gap after a big line.

**Music.** Know why it is there; if you can't say, leave it out. Music points, it doesn't decorate. Be specific (a style, an era, an instrument), never generic inspiring corporate. Under a voice, texture and pulse, no lyrics or busy melody. On a sad scene, music a little less sad than the picture. Short, rhythm-led videos cut to the music; story videos work dry first, then get scored. Bring a theme back at the end rather than a new track.

**In and out.** Start music at a turn, not at the top of a scene, and on a decision, not a slow fade. Under a voice, fade up so the middle of the fade lands on the first word. Pull the music out just before the line that matters. After a big line, let the music play alone for 4 to 7 seconds, with a change in it as the voice ends. Never end cold under speech, and no neat rimshot ending. In comedy, music comes after the punchline.

**Mix.** Levels, ducking and loudness live in `/invideo-timeline`.

**Effects.** Few and specific. One chosen detail beats six layers of ambience. Never library clichés: a hawk over every mountain, a beep on every keypress, a whoosh on every cut. Silence is room tone, never dead air, and it lands after sound.

**Cleanup.** Remove the noise, keep the person. Gentle passes, and fix only what distracts. A steady hint of room beats a voice that sounds like a filter or a background that jumps at every cut.

**Dubbing.** Adapt, don't translate: the line should sound natural in that language. Performance over perfect lips; sync matters most on close-ups and on p, b and m. Match the room so the voice sits with the music. For interviews, a translated voice over the lowered original keeps the real person.

## Rules

Keep the raw clip on a branch before a dialogue clean.
