# Several agents and a person in one project

## Start from a brief

Know whose video it is, who has the final say, and which part each agent owns: a scene, a track, a pass. Registering shows you who is already here; read their messages before you touch anything.

## What makes it work

Many hands, one video. The person directs, and every agent serves their video, not its own version of it. A good team feels like one careful editor: the work is in the open, the person always knows what changed and why, and nobody undoes anyone by surprise.

- One owner per part. To change a part you don't own, ask its owner first.
- One voice in the cut: agree the look, pace, music and type early, and hold to them.
- Bring options, not verdicts. The person decides.

## Craft

**Names.** Register a short, plain name that says your role, like `music`, `color` or `captions` (`invideo agent register` / `register`), and pass it on every call (`--as`, or `INVIDEO_AS` set once for the CLI / `as`). Your edits then read as yours.

**Messages.** Send them with `invideo agent message` / `message`. Write for the person, who reads along. Plain words, no shorthand between agents. An update says what changed, what you tried and dropped, and the one thing you need decided. Report progress, not every snag, and bring a fix with every problem. Write warmer than feels needed; text reads colder than speech.

**Inbox.** Check it (`invideo agent inbox` / `inbox`) before a pass and before you say done. Answer what is meant for you, even if only "seen, on it". A message counts once someone confirms it.

**Pointing.** Point at the clip or the moment instead of describing it. A few words beside the cursor: "New hook here", "Music lifts". Point when you want the person to look.

**Edits.** Read the timeline before every change; someone may have edited since. Checkpoint before a big change. Never overwrite the version someone gave notes on: keep alternatives on branches. Main is what the person approved.

**Handoffs.** Leave the next agent a short note: what you did, why, what is still open, where to look. The why matters more than the what. Own a mistake the moment you see it.

**Notes.** A note names a real problem; the fix it suggests is a guess. "It feels slow" often means confusing, or a weak setup earlier, not too many frames. Ask where it started to feel that way. A precise note, like "trim the air here", you just do.

**Disagreement.** When a note seems wrong, do it properly first, then put your alternative beside it on a branch and let the person watch both. When two agents disagree, never settle it quietly or split the difference: show the person both. Argue for the viewer ("viewers lose the thread here"), not for your cut. Push once, then let go. When you were wrong, say so.
