# Launch and product videos

## Start from a brief

Know the product, who it is for, the one thing they should remember, and where the video will play. Gather what the person has: the URL, screenshots, screen recordings, logo and brand colors.

## What makes it work

Earn attention in the first seconds: open on the product already moving, not a logo or title card. Lead with what is different; if a competitor could swap in their logo, it isn't done. Understanding beats visual density: motion should point the eye, not just keep things busy. Emotion and clarity work together; a big reveal means little if viewers can't say what launched.

- Show the real product: their screenshots and recordings.
- One idea per scene. Zoom in on the part of the screen that matters.
- Prefer specific claims and numbers over words like seamless or powerful.

## Craft

**Music.** Pick it first and cut to it. Choose what the company would play at its own party, never ukulele and claps or a stock riser into a drop. Keep it under the picture, and pull it out for half a second before the reveal.

**Voice.** The voiceover sounds like a person telling a friend: calm, a little understated, never an excited announcer. Never repeat on screen what the voice says.

**Cuts.** Hard cuts, plus one signature transition. Glitches, whips and light leaks once at most. Cut when an action or thought completes, not on a timer or every beat.

**Tension.** Each beat tops the last. Quick cuts build, a beat of quiet, then the biggest moment, about two-thirds through, on the music's peak. End calm: the product and the URL, held long enough to read twice, as the sound fades. Never a logo fade-out.

**Sound.** Three or four low, tactile sounds (click, whoosh, chime, impact), each landing a frame early. If whooshes are hiding weak cuts, fix the cuts.

## By type

| Video | Focus |
|---|---|
| Launch | The problem, the product, the outcome, the next step. |
| Feature demo | One feature, start to finish, in the real product. |
| Release notes | One change per scene. The same look every week. |
| Article to video | The few key points, not every paragraph. |
| Video sales letter | One offer, carried by the script. Slides, b-roll or an avatar on top. |
| Employer brand | Real employees in their own words. A 30 second cut for the job ad. |
