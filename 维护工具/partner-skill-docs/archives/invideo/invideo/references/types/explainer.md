# Explainers and courses

## Start from a brief

Know who is learning, what they already know, and the one idea they should be able to use afterwards. Gather the source: the deck, document, syllabus or script.

## What makes it work

Judge it by what the viewer can do afterwards, not how smooth it felt; the frictionless video often teaches least. Open on the problem, never the agenda: by 30 seconds the viewer knows what puzzle this solves. Show one concrete case before the rule, so the definition names something they have already seen.

- One idea per scene, one place to look at a time.
- Say the common wrong idea out loud, let it stand a beat, then break it.
- Link ideas with "but" and "so", never "and then". A list is not an explanation.

## Craft

**Screen.** Never put the narration's sentences on screen. The voice carries the sentence; the screen shows the thing, with two or three key words at most. Labels sit on the object, never in a legend. Build each frame one element at a time and dim what is done.

**Sync.** A reveal or highlight lands on the word that names it, not the sentence. Early, it is read alone; late, the voice has moved on.

**Voice.** Warm and conversational, talking to one person. Brisk is fine, flat is not. Cut ums and dead air; keep the beat after a surprise and before a key definition. Text to read gets silence.

**Pace.** Uneven on purpose: quick through the familiar, slow at the hard turn. Before an answer, pose the question and hold two seconds.

**Music.** Between ideas, never under the hard part. Drop it under dense explanation; bring it back at section breaks and the end.

**Motion.** Every move points the eye. When one thing becomes another, morph it; a cut says "new thing". The same idea keeps its color and its place on screen. No drifting pans to fight boredom.

**Endings.** Return to the opening problem or image, now understood. Never a bullet summary.

## By type

| Video | Focus |
|---|---|
| Slides to video | Pace by the voice, not the slide. Headlines become claims. Cut the agenda slide. |
| Document to explainer | The few points that matter, each with its own picture. |
| Update one line | Only that scene changes; voice, look and timing elsewhere stay put. |
| Online course | One idea per lesson, cut at concept boundaries. The same opening and close every lesson. |
| Whiteboard | Each drawing completes as its noun is spoken. The final board reads as a summary. |
| Translation | Retime the cut to the new voice, never speed the voice up. |

## Rules

Check the script against the source.
