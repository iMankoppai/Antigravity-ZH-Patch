# Captions

## Start from a brief

Know the job: social captions for a muted feed, accessible captions for viewers who cannot hear, or subtitles in another language. They share mechanics but not values. Captions are burned into the video; invideo exports no SRT or VTT file.

## What makes it work

A caption is an edit, not a transcript. Build it on the timeline: words and times from the script or a transcript (`invideo media audio.transcript` / `media`; it costs credits), one `add_captions` clip, then edit its slides. Every word read is attention taken from the picture. Good captions feel like watching, not reading.

- Break on meaning, never on width or word count. Pause on any frame: the text should make sense alone.
- Check names, numbers, brand terms and every "not" against the audio.
- One style from first card to last: case, brackets, colors, labels.

## Craft

**Timing.** In on the first frame of the voice. If speech starts within half a second of a cut, start on the cut; never hang a few frames across one. Gaps are 2 frames or half a second, nothing between, or the screen flickers. Hold half a second past the last word. Split before a punchline so it lands with the audio, not before.

**Reading.** About 20 characters a second and two lines of 42 are ceilings, not targets. Shorter top line. Viewers forgive fast text sooner than cut text or bad sync; when you must trim, drop filler, never paraphrase.

**Placement.** Never over a face, mouth, product, lower third or on-screen text. On vertical, lower-middle third, clear of the bottom and right edges. When other text is up, captions give way.

**Type.** Heavy sans, mixed case, a stroke or shadow that survives both a white shirt and a dark wall. All caps only on very short cards. Match the speaker: loud pop captions on a grief story read like an ad.

**Rhythm.** On social, the first card is the hook, on screen from frame one. Short cards for the hook, then full phrases as ideas get denser. The payoff gets its own card. Pop on the stressed syllable. At most one highlight per card, on the word the speaker stresses, in one color that keeps its meaning. Cut filler, keep the voice: if they say "dude", the caption says "dude".

**Sound.** Caption meaning, not noise: [laughs], [phone buzzing], [silence] when the moment turns on it. Specific beats vague: [tense strings], not [music]. Tag tone only when the face doesn't show it. If the sound was made hard to hear on purpose, write [muffled]; don't transcribe it.

## By type

| Video | Focus |
|---|---|
| Social | Works on mute: the hook card, rhythm, one emphasis. |
| ADA and Section 508 | Equivalence: the same jokes, scares and turns at the same moment. Speakers by position first, a label only when unclear, never a name before the story gives one. Lyrics verbatim between ♪. |
| LMS | Calm pacing, full sentences. Edit for young learners, never into simpler words. |
| Translated subtitles | Carry the effect, not the words. A joke lands on first read; swap a reference for one with the same weight. Match swearing and formality in strength. Re-chunk and re-time for each language. Subtitles are not accessible captions; ship both. |
