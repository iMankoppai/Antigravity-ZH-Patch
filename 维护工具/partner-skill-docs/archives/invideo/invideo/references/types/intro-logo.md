# Intros, logos and overlays

## Start from a brief

Know the channel, how its videos open today, and where each asset will play. Gather the logo as a clean file, the brand colors and fonts, and a few real frames the graphics will sit on.

## What makes it work

These pieces play hundreds of times to the same people. Design for the fiftieth viewing, not the first. The best branding is the shortest thing that is still unmistakable, and a logo animation can't make a channel feel premium; the videos do that. The more often something plays, the shorter and quieter it should be.

- One system, not one hero clip: fix the palette, one or two fonts, one signature move and one sound. Let the rest vary.
- Make the brand from the channel's own material. Template effects look rented.

## Craft

**Intros.** The hook comes first, then a 1 to 2 second sting, then the content. Never pause the voice or the action for the brand. Length is earned only by a returning audience.

**Logo.** Start from the final frame: the exact static logo, held long enough to read. Then design the way in, from what the mark is made of: strokes draw on, blocks assemble. If another logo could be swapped in, start again. One hero moment, never a shatter, glitch or light sweep for its own sake.

**Sound.** Half the logo. One signature of a few notes or a single designed hit, reused in the intro, stingers and outro. The visual lock lands on the sound's hit. Mix it under dialogue level and make a quiet version for serious episodes.

**Lower thirds.** The name is the boss; the role is smaller or lighter. Bring it in a beat after the speaker starts and the shot settles, take it out before the next cut. Arrive with ease out, leave faster, same place every time. Put it on the side the speaker faces away from, and design the backing for the worst shot in the series.

**Overlays.** The content is the hero. Frames and bugs are small, low contrast and nearly still. Save motion for alerts and keep those brief.

**Stingers.** The shortest, most restrained piece in the kit. The frame must fully cover the screen at the cut point; build that frame first and animate out both ways. Fill it with the brand's shapes, and keep the sound soft.

**Loops.** No climax and no hard accents. The last frame flows into the first in position and speed. Slow drift, long period. A starting soon screen says what starts and when.

**Outros.** Never a goodbye card. Keep the content going while the end screen appears, with calm space where its buttons sit.

## By type

| Video | Focus |
|---|---|
| Intro, logo animation | A 1 to 2 second sting plus a longer version from the same end frame. |
| Lower thirds, overlays | Quiet, identical motion, the same every time. |
| Stinger | Full cover at the cut point, under a second of real interruption. |
| Spotify Canvas | A seamless vertical loop that feels like the song. No text. |
| Quote or nature loop | One line, held to read twice, over slow motion with no visible seam. |
