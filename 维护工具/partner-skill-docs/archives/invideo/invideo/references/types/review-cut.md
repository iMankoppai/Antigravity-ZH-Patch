# Footage into a reviewable cut

## Start from a brief

Know the one feeling the viewer should leave with and where it will play. Gather all the footage, their music if they have it, and anything that must or must not appear.

## What makes it work

A cut is a selection, not a summary. Decide the feeling first, then pick and order moments to produce it. Emotion beats clean continuity: a cut that feels right with a jump in it beats a seamless one that feels like nothing. Ignore the source order; the best ending often sits early in the footage.

- On the first watch, note where you leaned in, laughed or got bored. Those moments are the cut.
- Every shot has a job: feeling, information, rhythm or contrast. Looking nice is not a job.
- Drop anything that only works if you know where it came from.

## Craft

**Music.** Pick it first and cut to it. Choose a track with lows and peaks; a flat track gives the cut nowhere to go. Edit it on phrase boundaries, never mid-bar. One track for a short recap. Dip it fast under a spoken line, about 4 frames, and bring it back slowly.

**Cuts.** Hard cuts. Cut in just before the action and out right after the payoff, never after the hit has landed. Sync by feel: a cut a frame or two early often reads more in time than one on the waveform. Don't cut on every beat; vary how long shots hold.

**Tension.** Escalate, then stop. Never open at full energy. Bring it down for a beat before each peak, and put the biggest moment on the music's biggest moment. End on the strongest beat, not a slow fade.

**Sound.** People reacting sell the picture: the yell, the gasp, the laugh, the room applauding. A beat of silence before the big moment hits harder than a riser.

**Restraint.** Every effect, ramp and flash needs a reason. Show a failure or a close call; a run of only wins goes numb.

**Review.** Hand over a cut that plays, not an assembly. A note says where something felt wrong, rarely why; the cause is often earlier, or the music. Try their version first. Branch before each big change so every version they saw survives.

## By type

| Video | Focus |
|---|---|
| Trailer or teaser | Sell the feeling, not the plot. Escalate, end on a question, a button after the title. |
| Dailies | Selects and string-outs: every take of a moment side by side. |
| Event recap | How the room felt. Arrival, build, peak, close. Faces over stages and slides. |
| Gaming montage | The play is the star. Map the song first; the best play on the biggest drop. |
| AI clip | Finish it: sound, music, type and pacing around the shot. |
