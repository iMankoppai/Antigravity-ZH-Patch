# Interviews, podcasts and talking heads

## Start from a brief

Know who will watch and what should stay with them. Gather every take, the script if there was one, and any footage of what people talk about.

## What makes it work

When people talk, the editor is the writer. Topic is not story: a customer covering their onboarding is information; the night they almost quit is the film. Say what it is about in one sentence; keep only what pulls toward it. On the first listen, note where you leaned in, laughed or stopped believing them. Those are the lines.

- Search the transcript, choose by ear. Text hides a rising pitch that can't end anything.
- Spend their screen time on what only they can say: what it felt like, what they got wrong. Facts go to on-screen text.
- Specific beats general. A small admission beats a big claim.

## Craft

**Structure.** Alternate a moment and what it meant. Join bites with "but" and "so", never "and then". The problem has to hurt before the result means anything. Open on tension, not a bio; a cold open raises a question, it doesn't spend the ending. End on change or a reflection, never a sales line, and end once.

**Cuts.** Cut where a thought ends, on a falling pitch, not where a sentence ends. Cut after a breath, never on a stressed word. Keep the speaker's own rhythm; a slow talker never turns rapid-fire.

**Silence.** Cut the empty pause, keep the full one. After the line that matters, hold two or three seconds so it rings. Keep a few ums: clean the stumble of searching for a word, keep the hesitation of deciding to say it.

**Picture.** The face for confessions, b-roll for explanation. B-roll shows the exact thing they describe, never generic wallpaper. An honest jump cut beats a cutaway that only hides a join.

**Music.** Under setups and transitions, out under the line that matters. Never sad music under a sad moment. The voice always sits on top.

**Truth.** Every answer stays with its real question. Never invent a pause or a reaction, or imply a cause that isn't true.

## By type

| Video | Focus |
|---|---|
| Testimonial | Before, decision, after. The person is the hero; the product enters late. |
| Case study | Give each voice a job: stakes, detail, proof. Let one answer another. |
| Documentary | Interviews are the spine. When there's footage of it, show it instead. |
| Podcast | Keep the laughs and real pauses. Clips stand alone: open on the answer, end on the landing. |
| Webinar replay | Cut the waiting. Open on the insight, promote the best audience question, add chapters. |
| Long YouTube | Deliver the title's promise in the first minute. Fix slow stretches with structure, not zooms. |
| Script assembly | The best-delivered take of each line, not the first clean one. |

## Rules

The cut must keep the speaker's meaning.
