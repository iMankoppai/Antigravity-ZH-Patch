# Occasion slideshow

The recipe birthdays, weddings, memorials, anniversaries and invitations share: a pile of photos and clips from many people, cut to music for a room full of people who know the person.

## The pile

Hundreds or thousands of photos come in; a few dozen matter. Sort in passes. First drop what nobody would miss: blur that hides the moment, backs of heads, near-duplicates. Of near-duplicates, keep the better face, not the sharper photo. Then pick for meaning: the photos the family would grab in a fire. Ask who must appear, and check that they do.

## Order

Decide the first and last photo before anything else. A life runs in time order; an event runs by mood, in chapters. Give each chapter its own stretch of the song, and change chapters on a musical turn.

## Pacing

Every move has a reason: push in toward a face, pull out to reveal who was there. Some photos never move. Vary time by weight: a quick burst from one party, then a portrait held six seconds. Fewer photos, held longer, beat a fast flood.

## Music

Choose it for the person, never generic piano. For older people, the songs of their youth, roughly ages 10 to 30. One track, two at most. Put the film's peak where the song builds. Lyrics never caption the picture.

Clear the track for where it will play: a song for one room and a public upload are different rights. Rights-cleared tracks from the library carry no surprise later.

## Many contributors

Ask everyone for the same thing: one short clip, held steady, answering one question. Level every voice to the same loudness, and trim the "is it recording?" from both ends.

## The venue loop

A screen at the venue needs its own cut: no voice it depends on, because the room is loud. Hold each photo longer, skip the title cards, and make the last frame flow into the first so nobody sees the seam. Test the file on the screen it will play on before the day.
